## 1. Vision (recap)

A chemistry-driven **materials & processing framework**. Materials are data with real
properties (e.g. magnet strength: NdFeB vs SmCo). Ores follow realistic
**raw → beneficiation → intermediate → metal/product** chains. Everything is **tag-driven**
so other mods (and our own groups) consume outputs without caring about the source.
**A Create add-on: Create is required, NeoForge only** (user ruling 2026-10-05; Create has no
Fabric release for 1.21.1). Developed **separately from wildspell** (may interop
via shared tags).

Source data: **`data/ores.json`** (113 ores, 74 commodities, 49 processing stages),
human view in **`docs/ore-catalog.md`**, regenerated by `tools/build_catalog.py`.

## 2. Architecture

### 2.1 Material system
- A `Material` = element / compound / alloy / mineral with properties, driven from data.
- Fields: `id`, `display`, `type`, `formula`, `props{ density, magnetStrength,
  heatResistance, conductivity, hardness, radioactivity, toxicity }`, `forms[]`
  (ore, raw, concentrate, oxide, dust, ingot, nugget, plate, …), `tags[]`.
- **Magnet strength is a first-class property** — machines read the stat, so NdFeB and SmCo
  differ mechanically. Leaves an API seam for a future field simulation (don't block on it).

### 2.2 Processing / stage system
- The **49 canonical stages** (`data/ores.json.stageVocabulary`) each become a machine /
  recipe type. Recipes are data-driven: `input item|tag → stage machine (tier) →
  output(s) + byproducts`.
- A chain is the ordered `stages[]` on an ore entry. Byproducts branch to other materials.

### 2.3 Tier progression
| Tier | Theme | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Hand | Crushing (manual) |
| T1 | Mechanical | Crushing, Grinding, GravitySeparation, MagneticSeparation, ElectrostaticSeparation, FrothFlotation, Washing, Screening |
| T2 | Thermal | Roasting, Calcination, Sintering, Smelting, Converting, Decrepitation, Volatilization, Chlorination, CarbothermicReduction |
| T3 | Hydromet | Leaching, Dissolution, Precipitation, Evaporation, Crystallization, PressureOxidation, BioOxidation, CarbonAdsorption |
| T4 | Electro & light-metal | Electrowinning, Electrolysis, HallHeroult, Electrorefining, Cupellation, named processes (Bayer/Solvay/Contact/Claus/Frasch/Siemens) |
| T5 | Advanced separation (endgame) | SolventExtraction cascade, IonExchange → individual REEs; KrollProcess / MoltenSaltElectrolysis / MetallothermicReduction; PGM refining; Alloying |

Endgame crux = REE element-by-element separation, Kroll titanium, PGM six-metal refining.

### 2.4 Minerals in the ground, elements as products  *(ruling by A, 2026-10-04)*
There is no such substance as "iron ore" — ore is rock carrying a mineral at mineable grade. The
mod follows that:

- **Only minerals generate in the world** (hematite, magnetite, chalcopyrite, sphalerite,
  bastnäsite…). Vanilla iron ore generation is replaced by them; a mod
  like Create's zinc ore is replaced by sphalerite.
- **Gold and copper are the exceptions:** native gold and native copper are real minerals, so
  vanilla gold ore and vanilla copper ore *are* those minerals — they stay and keep generating,
  and we register no block for them (`registry.OreBlocks.VANILLA`).
- **Elements have no `ORE` or `RAW` form.** They exist only as products (dust, ingot, nugget,
  plate, block, oxide). A mineral is a `MaterialType.MINERAL` material carrying `ORE`/`RAW`.
- **Vanilla items are the canonical form where one exists** (`minecraft:iron_ingot`, nugget,
  block; same for copper and gold). Never register a duplicate; the registration layer maps the
  form to the vanilla item.
- **Minerals carry the commodity tag**: hematite and magnetite are in `c:ores/iron`, so other
  mods' recipes keep working.
- **Primitive routes are the historical ones**, and they set the early game (T0, no machines):

  | Metal | Mineral | Ancient route | Catch |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Iron | hematite, magnetite, goethite | Bloomery: mineral + charcoal → solid spongy **bloom** (iron + slag, never melts) → hammer to wrought iron | Low yield, slag loss; a blast furnace later makes molten pig iron, which needs converting to steel |
  | Copper | malachite, cuprite (oxidised zone) | Direct charcoal smelt | Sulfides (chalcopyrite) must be roasted first — that's the T2 route |
  | Tin | cassiterite | Direct charcoal smelt | Rare, placer/pegmatite only |
  | Lead / silver | galena | Roast-smelt in an open fire; cupellation frees the silver | Toxic |
  | Gold | native gold | Panning / washing, then melt | No chemistry needed |
  | Zinc | sphalerite, smithsonite | **None** — zinc boils (907 °C) below its reduction temperature, so the metal escapes as vapour | Ancient brass was made by cementation (calamine + copper); zinc metal needs retort distillation (T2+) |

  Everything else (Al, Ti, REEs, PGMs, …) has no primitive route.

### 2.5 What can be processed when  *(direction from the user, 2026-10-05)*
Every mineral generates and can be mined from the start, but most cannot be turned into metal
until the player has machines. That is intended: the ore is a reason to build them.

| Era | How | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| **By hand** (built) | Bloomery + charcoal, smithing hammer, mortar and pestle | Iron from hematite / magnetite / goethite; mineral pigments. Vanilla copper and gold ore smelt as ever (native copper, native gold). |
| **By hand** (next) | The same bloomery, taking other oxide and carbonate minerals | Copper from malachite / azurite / cuprite; tin from cassiterite; lead from galena after roasting on a fire. Bronze follows. |
| **Early Create** | Crushing wheels, millstone, washing, mixing, pressing | Better yield from every hand route; crushed ore and byproducts; brass once zinc exists. |
| **Mid-game, Create-style multiblocks** | Roaster, blast furnace, flotation cells, retort, leach tanks | The sulfides (chalcopyrite, sphalerite, pentlandite, molybdenite), zinc (it boils before it reduces, so it needs a retort), steel, aluminium from bauxite, nickel, chromium, tungsten, titanium. |
| **Late game** | Solvent-extraction batteries, electrolysis, precious-metal refinery | The rare earths one element at a time, cobalt, the platinum group, mercury. |

Until an ore's route exists it is still worth finding: several grind to dye, and Fortune and
core-grade ore mean a deposit marked early pays back later.

## 4. Cohesion conventions (keep both sides compatible)

**Packages** (root `ai.gsmc.fundamentals`):
`.material` · `.process` · `.content.<group_slug>` · `.worldgen` · `.datagen` ·
`.integration.create` · `.registry`.

**Group slugs** (identical to `docs/research/` and §3): `ferrous_ferroalloy`, `base_metals`,
`industrial_minerals`, `light_battery_tech`, `precious_pgm`, `rare_earths`, `minor_specialty`.
Use as sub-package names and data sub-folders.

**IDs:**
- material id = element symbol lowercased or snake_case compound: `iron`, `neodymium`,
  `tungsten_trioxide`, `rare_earth_concentrate`.
- ore block id = `<mineral>_ore`, one block per mineral. It carries two blockstate properties:
  `grade` (`core` / `edge` / `trace`) and `host` (the rock it formed in: stone, deepslate, granite,
  diorite, andesite, tuff, calcite, dripstone, sand, our four rocks, and Create's seven stones).
  The mineral is a transparent texture drawn over the host rock's own texture, so there are no
  `deepslate_` twins. Bauxite, the laterites, REE clay and bog iron are whole rocks instead.
- stage machine id = snake_case of the canonical stage: `froth_flotation`, `solvent_extraction`.

**Tags** (Fabric `c:` common convention + our namespace; consumers request by tag, never by a
concrete item): `c:ores/<commodity>`, `c:raw_materials/<mineral>`, `c:ingots/<metal>`,
`c:dusts/<material>`, `fundamentals:concentrates/<commodity>`,
`fundamentals:oxides/<material>`, `fundamentals:stages/<stage>`.

**Data & registration (conflict avoidance):**
- Each group registers through its **own** registrar class (`FerrousContent`, `RareEarthContent`,
  …) called from common init — so parallel diffs never touch a shared central registry body.
- Content JSON lives under `src/main/resources/data/fundamentals/<group_slug>/…`. **Never edit
  another group's content.**
- **Shared schema files** (material schema, stage/recipe schema, tier defs, the
  `data/ores.json` structure, this PLAN) are **defined once and are append-only** — change them
  only via PR + a note in §6. B owns creating these first (see §6).
- NeoForge only. Stonecutter stays for future Minecraft versions; there are no loader branches.

**Branches/PRs:** feature branches `a/<group>-<topic>` or `b/<group>-<topic>`
(e.g. `b/ferrous-smelting`, `a/ree-separation`). PR into `main`; keep each PR within your own
group's files. No direct content commits to `main`.

## 5. Worldgen / biome spawn scheme

Spawn rates vary widely, as in reality: placement is by real **deposit type → biome + depth +
rarity**. Both machines use this table so the world is coherent regardless of who owns the ore.

**Rarity:** Common · Uncommon · Rare · VeryRare · Anomaly(structure/vein).
**Depth bands:** Surface (placer/beach/river) · Upper (0..64) · Mid (-32..16) ·
Deepslate (-64..0) · Deep-anomaly.

| Deposit type | Real geology | MC biomes | Depth | Rarity | Example ores (owner) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Banded iron / cratonic | ancient shields, ubiquitous | nearly all land | Upper+Mid | Common | hematite, magnetite, taconite, goethite (B) |
| Porphyry Cu-Mo | volcanic arcs | mountains, windswept hills | Mid | Uncommon | chalcopyrite, bornite (A) + molybdenite (B) co-spawn |
| Laterite (weathering) | tropical, high rainfall | jungle, savanna, mangrove | Surface+Upper | Uncommon | bauxite (A), Ni-laterite limonite/saprolite (B) |
| Granite pegmatite / greisen | granitic highlands | mountains, windswept, badlands | Mid | Rare | cassiterite (A), wolframite (B), spodumene·beryl·coltan (B) |
| Carbonatite | rare alkaline intrusions | rare vein in mountains/mesa | Deep-anomaly | VeryRare | bastnäsite (A), pyrochlore-Nb (B) |
| Ion-adsorption clay | weathered subtropical granite | jungle / forested hills | Surface | Rare | heavy-REE clay, Dy/Tb (A) |
| Evaporite | arid basins, dry lakes | desert, badlands | Upper (layered) | Uncommon | halite, sylvite/potash, trona, borax, gypsum (B) |
| Placer / mineral sands | rivers, beaches | river, beach, stony shore | Surface | Uncommon | gold (B), ilmenite·rutile·zircon (B), monazite (A) |
| VMS / SEDEX Pb-Zn-Ag | marine sulfide basins | underground, caves | Mid+Deepslate | Uncommon | galena, sphalerite (A) |
| Hydrothermal vein | fault/volcanic | mountains, basalt deltas | Mid | Rare | stibnite·cinnabar (B), native Au·Ag quartz (B) |
| Sandstone / unconformity U | redbeds, basins | badlands, desert | Deepslate | VeryRare (radioactive) | carnotite, uraninite (B) |
| Layered mafic intrusion | rare, deep | deepslate vein | VeryRare | chromite + PGM (B) co-spawn |
| Marine phosphate | shelf sediments | beach/ocean-adjacent, swamp | Upper | Uncommon | apatite/phosphate rock (B) |

**Philosophy:** iron/copper/aluminium common; alloy metals uncommon; Li/Sn/W/REE rare and
biome-locked; PGM/U/heavy-REE very rare or anomaly-gated. Encode later as per-biome placed
features in worldgen JSON — for now this table is the spec both sides build to.
