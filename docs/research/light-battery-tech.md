# Light, Battery & Tech Metals — Ore & Processing Research

Scope: light metals, battery metals, and electronics/tech metals. Excludes rare earths, aluminium, and titanium (handled by other groups). Formulas and routes verified against current literature (see Sources at bottom).

## Per-commodity notes

**Lithium.** Two dominant primary routes. (1) Hard rock: spodumene is roasted ~1050 °C to convert inert α-spodumene to leachable β-spodumene (decrepitation, ~30% volume expansion), then "acid roast" with concentrated H₂SO₄ at ~250 °C produces water-soluble Li₂SO₄; leach, remove Mg/Ca/Fe, and precipitate Li₂CO₃ with soda ash (or convert to LiOH). (2) Brine: pump salar brine into solar evaporation ponds (loses >90% water), remove Mg/Ca, then precipitate Li₂CO₃ with Na₂CO₃. Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE, sorption/IX) is an emerging alternative to ponds. Lepidolite/petalite are minor hard-rock sources; lepidolite carries Rb/Cs/K credits.

**Beryllium.** Bertrandite (higher Be%, ~0.1–0.5% Be in US ore but the dominant mined source at Spor Mountain, UT) is leached directly with H₂SO₄. Beryl (gem-grade excluded; industrial beryl ~4–5% Be) must first be melted and quenched ("fritted") to make it acid-reactive. Both converge on a BeSO₄ liquor refined by solvent extraction (D2EHPA) and ammonium-carbonate complexation to Be(OH)₂, then to metal, BeO, or Cu-Be master alloy.

**Tantalum & Niobium.** Coltan concentrate is digested in HF to form soluble heptafluoride complexes H₂TaF₇/H₂NbF₇; solvent extraction (MIBK) exploits the different stabilities to split Ta from Nb, giving K₂TaF₇ / Ta₂O₅ and Nb₂O₅. Ta metal from Na reduction of K₂TaF₇ or electron-beam melting. Niobium's bulk source is pyrochlore (Brazil/Canada), processed mainly to ferroniobium by aluminothermic reduction for steel alloying.

**Magnesium.** >80% of primary Mg today is the Pidgeon (silicothermic) process: calcine dolomite/magnesite to oxide, then reduce with ferrosilicon under vacuum at ~1200 °C — Mg vapor condenses as crown metal. The electrolytic route (molten MgCl₂ → Mg + Cl₂) is used where cheap power/brine exists (seawater-derived Mg(OH)₂ → MgCl₂).

**Caesium / Rubidium.** Pollucite (Tanco mine, Manitoba is the classic source) is acid-digested; CsCl and high-value caesium formate (drilling fluid) are the main products. Rb almost always occurs with Cs (and in lepidolite) and is separated by fractional crystallisation of alums or solvent extraction — no economic primary Rb ore.

**Zirconium / Hafnium.** Zircon (a heavy-mineral-sands byproduct alongside ilmenite/rutile) is carbochlorinated to ZrCl₄, which carries ~2% HfCl₄. For nuclear-grade Zr (low neutron cross-section) Hf must be removed by solvent extraction (MIBK/thiocyanate or TBP) or extractive distillation; both metals are then made by Kroll (Mg) reduction of the tetrachloride to sponge. Most zircon is used as-is (foundry sand, opacifier, ZrO₂) rather than reduced to metal.

## Byproduct metals (no primary ore) — cross-links

These tech metals have essentially no standalone primary ore; they are recovered as byproducts of base-metal refining and should be modeled as cross-links from those chains, not as independent ore entries:

- **Gallium** — from bauxite (Bayer alumina liquor) and from zinc processing.
- **Germanium** — from zinc refining (sphalerite residues) and some coal fly ash.
- **Indium** — from zinc refining (sphalerite), recovered from flue dusts/residues.
- **Selenium** and **Tellurium** — from copper electrorefining anode slimes.
- **Rhenium** — from molybdenite (MoS₂) roaster flue dust, itself a Cu-porphyry byproduct.
